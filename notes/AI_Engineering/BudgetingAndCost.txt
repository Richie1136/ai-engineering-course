# Budgeting and Cost in AI Development

## The Two Main Factors

The two main factors determining how powerful an AI model is are:

* Dataset Size
* Model Size

A larger, higher-quality dataset significantly enhances the model's learning potential, while the model's size determines its learning capacity.

---

## Dataset Size vs Model Size

Think of a large dataset as a library full of books.

The more high-quality books available in the library, the more knowledge can be learned.

In this analogy, model size can be compared to a student's learning capacity.

Students who can remember and internalize more information can make better use of a large library.

Similarly, a larger AI model with more parameters can capture more intricate patterns and make more complex decisions based on information learned from the dataset.

---

## Performance vs Cost

The larger an AI model's dataset and model size, the higher its performance can be expected to be.

However, building the largest model is not always the best strategy because there is a trade-off between performance and cost efficiency.

Challenges include:

* Acquiring large, high-quality datasets.
* Purchasing computing resources.
* Training large models.
* Operating large models after training.

---

## Why Budgeting Matters

Budgeting is a crucial topic and a fundamental skill AI developers need to acquire.

You should not start building an AI model and then decide how large it should be or how much it will cost during training.

Instead, it is better to determine the available budget beforehand and decide how much will be allocated to:

* Data acquisition
* Computing power

---

## Real-World Costs

Training GPT-4 cost OpenAI over $100 million.

Most organizations cannot afford multiple attempts at training AI models.

As a result, they must find the right balance between:

* Model effectiveness
* Cost efficiency

---

## Bigger Is Not Always Better

Bigger AI models require more computing power during both training and operation.

Creating a larger model should only be justified when the increase in effectiveness aligns with the organization's goals and resources.

In some situations, building smaller models that can be retrained and fine-tuned frequently makes more sense.

Training smaller, specialized models is often:

* More cost-effective
* Faster to train

than using large amounts of resources to train a massive model for limited applications.
